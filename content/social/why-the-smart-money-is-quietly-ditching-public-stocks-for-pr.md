## LinkedIn

A farmer sells 320 acres, a wealth manager pitches a private credit fund with a quarterly capital call, and the K-1 doesn't show up until September. That's the pattern we're seeing in Brookings this year, and it creates real problems: taxable income can get allocated before any cash lands in your account, and a fund working across ten states can mean ten state returns on one modest allocation. Farm and ag clients already juggle K-1s from an operating LLC, a land entity, and equipment leasing. Add a private equity sleeve and you've got five K-1s with five different timelines. Julie Underwood, CPA, models the state footprint and phantom income exposure before capital gets committed, not after the surprise bill in April. If a private credit or private equity pitch has landed in your inbox, read the full breakdown here: https://trucountcpa.com/resources/why-the-smart-money-is-quietly-ditching-public-stocks-for-pr
