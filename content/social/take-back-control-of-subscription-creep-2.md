## LinkedIn

A grain marketing app at $79 a month. A yield-mapping tool nobody's opened since 2023. A payroll add-on that outlived its usefulness two upgrades ago. Stack those charges together on a farm with three entities, or a service business with a dozen employees, and you're often looking at $400 to $1,200 a month in subscriptions nobody's using.

We call it subscription creep, and it hides well in ag and professional service books because every entity, every field, every new hire adds another login that rarely gets canceled. The fix isn't complicated: pull 90 days of statements, assign an owner to each charge, and check actual usage instead of good intentions from signup day.

One more thing worth knowing: if those precision-ag subscriptions are tied to on-farm trials, the invoices might support an R&D tax credit claim. Keep them.
