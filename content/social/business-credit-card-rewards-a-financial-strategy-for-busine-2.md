## LinkedIn

A $2,000 equipment purchase paid with a rewards card still deducts as $2,000, not $1,960, even after you earn $40 in cash back. That's the deduction mix-up we catch most often when we look at a new client's books here in Brookings.

Business credit card rewards aren't taxable income when they come from actual spending, but the card matters less than whether it matches your real spend. A farm running $60,000 a year in diesel and parts needs a fuel-focused card. A professional service firm with $30,000 in software and travel costs needs something else entirely.

Our latest post walks through the framework we use with agribusiness and service clients to match card type to spend pattern, and how to keep rewards from muddying your monthly financials.
