## LinkedIn

A Brookings-area service business switched credit cards last year and picked up roughly $900 in redeemable rewards, with zero change to actual spending. The move worked because their new card paid 3x on software and office purchases, the category that made up nearly 40% of their annual spend. Most business owners never run that math. A farm spending $40,000 a year on fuel could be leaving $600 on the table with a flat 1.5% card instead of a 3% fuel card. Rewards earned on normal purchases are generally not taxable, but sign-up bonuses without a purchase requirement can be treated as income, and that distinction changes how a bookkeeper should record it. Julie Underwood, CPA, and the TruCount team reconcile these rewards as part of ongoing bookkeeping, not a scramble in March.
