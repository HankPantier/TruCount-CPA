## LinkedIn

A CPA who volunteers 20 hours prepping a nonprofit's books can't deduct a dollar of that labor. Neither can a contractor who frames a Habitat for Humanity house on a Saturday. The IRS has never allowed a deduction for the value of time, no matter the skill or the market rate.

But the mileage to get there, at 14 cents per mile, and the supplies you buy and donate, like feed for a 4-H demonstration barn, often are deductible if you itemize and keep a written log. For farmers and ag operators serving on co-op boards or donating grain to a church fundraiser, the classification matters even more: donated grain can avoid triggering income altogether, while cash from a grain sale doesn't get the same treatment.

Julie Underwood, CPA, breaks down what qualifies, what doesn't, and how to document it before the IRS asks.
