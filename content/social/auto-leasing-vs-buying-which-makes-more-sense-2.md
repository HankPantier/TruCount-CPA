## LinkedIn

A $55,000 crew cab truck used 80% for hauling feed and equipment can cut a farmer's tax bill by $8,000 to $12,000 in year one, but only if it's bought instead of leased. Buy it, and Section 179 depreciation front-loads that deduction. Lease it, and you only deduct the business-use portion of payments as they're made, spread thin over the term.

For farm and ag operators logging 30,000 to 50,000 miles a year hauling grain and livestock, lease mileage caps get blown past almost every year, and overage fees running $0.15 to $0.25 per mile turn a good-looking payment into an expensive mistake by year two. Professional service owners driving under 10,000 miles a year for client meetings often land on the opposite answer.

The right call depends on your entity structure, mileage, and tax bracket, not the payment that looks smallest today.
