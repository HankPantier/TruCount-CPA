## LinkedIn

A portfolio that started at 60% stocks and 40% bonds can quietly drift to 75/25 in three or four years, just from market gains. Nobody decided to take on that extra risk. It happened anyway.

For farmers and business owners in Brookings, that drift matters more than it does for most investors, because 70-80% of net worth is often already sitting in land, equipment, or the business itself. Rebalancing an outside brokerage account without checking that math just repeats a concentration risk you already carry.

There's also a tax layer most people miss: selling appreciated stock to rebalance triggers a gain, and timing that gain against a strong crop year (or a deferred grain contract) can change your tax bill more than the rebalance itself. Julie Underwood, CPA, works through these timing questions with our ag and business clients.
