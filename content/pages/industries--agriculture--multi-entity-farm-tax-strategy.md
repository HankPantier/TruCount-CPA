## Why farm plus service company combos need a different tax plan

Running a farm alongside a custom application business, equipment leasing operation, or ag consulting practice means managing two or more sets of books, two tax filings, and a pile of shared expenses that don't sort themselves neatly by entity. Most tax preparers treat each business as a standalone client: the farm gets a Schedule F, the service company gets a corporate return, and nobody looks at how the two interact.

That's where the real savings disappear. A tractor leased from the equipment company to the farm, labor shared between a custom harvesting crew and a row-crop operation, or fertilizer expense that could sit on either set of books, these decisions change your total tax bill every year.

Julie Underwood, CPA, and the TruCount team look at farm and service entities as one connected picture, not two separate files. That's the difference between filing taxes and building a strategy around every operation your family runs across Brookings, Kingsbury, and Moody counties.

## Choosing the right structure for your farm and related businesses

An LLC taxed as a partnership works fine for one farm. Add a custom spraying business, a trucking operation, or a hunting lease, and the calculus changes fast. Should the equipment sit in a separate LLC and lease back to the farm? Does the service business make more sense as an S-corp to manage self-employment tax on custom work income? Should family members hold ownership differently in the farm partnership than in the ag consulting practice?

Generic entity-selection videos and checklists cover LLC versus S-corp for a single business. They don't address what happens when your farm operation and your service business share equipment, employees, and even acreage. TruCount evaluates every entity you own together, not one at a time, so a change in one structure doesn't create a tax surprise in another.

Julie Underwood, CPA, a member of the AICPA and the South Dakota Society of CPAs, walks through the ownership, liability, and tax tradeoffs for each entity in your operation before recommending any change. [Learn more about entity type analysis](/services/entity-type-analysis).

## Uncovering deductions built for South Dakota agriculture

Generic ag deduction advice tells you to track fuel, feed, and fertilizer expenses. That's step one, not the strategy. South Dakota farm operations running multiple entities have access to deductions most local preparers never raise, because using them requires coordinating income and expenses across entities, not just filing one return.

- **R&D credit for farmers**: Custom formulation of seed treatments, new tillage or irrigation methods, and on-farm variety trials can qualify for the federal research and development credit. Few Brookings-area preparers raise this with row-crop or livestock operators.
- **Excess fertility deduction**: When you purchase farmland with fertilizer, lime, or nutrients already built into the soil, that value can often be deducted separately from the land basis instead of depreciated over decades.
- **Entity-level expense allocation**: Splitting shared equipment, fuel, and labor costs correctly across farm and service entities keeps deductions where they generate the most benefit.
- **Section 179 and bonus depreciation timing**: Coordinating equipment purchases across entities so the deduction lands in the year it does the most good.

## Coordinating cash flow and income timing across entities

Farm income is seasonal. Service business income, from custom application, equipment rental, or consulting, often isn't. When you can see both sets of books in the same place, you can time equipment purchases, prepay expenses, or shift income recognition between entities to smooth out a tax bill that would otherwise spike in a good crop year and crater in a bad one.

TruCount's secure online platform gives you real-time access to both the farm and service company financials side by side, so timing decisions don't wait on a phone call or an emailed spreadsheet. The recommendation to prepay input costs in November or hold equipment income into January comes with real numbers behind it, not a guess made in March.

## Planning ahead for family farm transitions

How you structure your farm and its related service businesses today shapes how easily the next generation steps into them. A farm partnership with clean entity lines, documented ownership percentages, and a service business held separately from the land is far easier to transition, gift, or sell into than a tangle of shared assets nobody ever wrote down.

TruCount isn't a succession planning firm, and no document replaces a conversation with your family. But the entity choices made now, who owns what percentage of the equipment company, whether the trucking operation sits inside the farm partnership or beside it, are the groundwork every future transition sits on. Get the structure right at 45, and the transition at 65 gets a lot simpler for everyone at the table.

## Frequently Asked Questions About Multi entity farm tax strategy

**Q: What is a multi-entity farm tax strategy?**
A: It's a tax plan that looks at a farm operation and any related businesses, like equipment leasing, custom application, or ag consulting, together rather than filing each entity separately. This coordinated view uncovers deductions and timing opportunities that generic, single-entity tax prep misses entirely.

**Q: What is the R&D credit for farmers?**
A: The federal research and development credit can apply to farm activities like custom seed treatment formulation, new tillage or irrigation methods, and on-farm variety trials. Most CPAs in Brookings don't raise it with row-crop or livestock operators, but it can meaningfully reduce a farm's tax liability.

**Q: What is the excess fertility deduction?**
A: When farmland is purchased with fertilizer, lime, or nutrients already built into the soil, that value can often be deducted separately from land basis rather than depreciated over decades. It's a South Dakota agriculture-specific deduction that requires proper valuation to claim correctly.
