## Plan your transition with confidence, not guesswork

Most professional services owners in Brookings don't think about their exit until a buyer calls, a partner wants out, or retirement suddenly feels closer than it used to. By then, you're reconstructing three years of financials instead of having a plan already in motion.

TruCount CPA PC works differently. Because we handle your bookkeeping, tax planning, and outsourced accounting year-round, we already know your numbers before succession becomes a conversation. That means when you're ready to talk about selling, transferring ownership, or bringing in a partner, we're not starting from zero. We're picking up a plan we've been building with you all along.

Whether you're weighing retirement in five years or fielding an offer this year, the goal is the same: a transition that protects what you've built and gives you real choices, not forced ones.

## Know what your business is worth before you decide to sell

A credible valuation starts with clean, current books, not a rush job the month a buyer shows interest. Businesses that treat bookkeeping as an afterthought often spend weeks reconstructing prior-year records before they can even start a valuation conversation, and that delay costs leverage at the negotiating table.

Our outsourced accounting relationships give service business owners in Brookings, Kingsbury, and Moody counties real-time financials through a secure online platform, so you can see your numbers whenever you need them, not just at tax time. When a buyer, partner, or family successor asks what the business is worth, you're not guessing. You're pointing to accurate, up-to-date books that reflect what you've actually built.

That foundation also matters if you're not selling. Partner buyouts and family transfers both hinge on a number everyone trusts, and trust starts with the accuracy of the underlying records.

## Build a succession plan that fits your timeline, not a template

There's no single right way to exit a service business, and a generic playbook rarely fits what you're actually trying to do. TruCount's year-round advisory work means we can talk through the option that matches your situation, not a one-size-fits-all checklist.

- **Family succession**, including how ownership transfers to a spouse or adult child without creating a tax burden that outweighs the benefit
- **Partner buyouts**, structured so the departing owner is paid fairly and the remaining owner isn't left cash-strapped
- **Third-party sale**, with financials organized well enough to withstand buyer due diligence
- **Gradual transition**, phasing out your role over one to three years while a successor steps in

Because we're already inside your books and your tax planning, we can model each option against your actual numbers instead of theoretical ones.

## Structure your exit to minimize tax surprises

Entity structure decisions made years ago can create expensive surprises at the exit table. An LLC taxed as a partnership, an S corporation, or a sole proprietorship each carry different tax treatment when ownership changes hands, and the wrong structure heading into a sale can mean a bigger tax bill than the owner expected.

Our entity type analysis work looks at your structure ahead of a transition, not during it, so there's time to make adjustments before they're locked in by a closing date. That might mean revisiting how the business is taxed, timing a sale across fiscal years, or planning around depreciation recapture before it becomes an unwelcome number on a closing statement.

This is where year-round tax planning pays off. Owners who only talk to a CPA once a year at filing time rarely have enough runway to make structural changes before a deal closes. Owners working with TruCount throughout the year do.

## Multi-entity and combined farm plus service company transitions

A fair number of the owners we work with in Brookings, Kingsbury, and Moody counties don't run just one business. It's common to see a professional services company alongside a farm or agribusiness operation, often with overlapping ownership, shared equipment, or intertwined cash flow between the two.

That combination makes succession more complicated, not less. A succession plan that only accounts for the service business while ignoring the farm entity next to it can create ownership gaps, unequal inheritances among children, or unexpected tax consequences when the two are separated. Our experience untangling multi-entity structures, including those that combine farm and service company interests, means we look at the full picture before recommending a path forward, not just the piece that's easiest to plan around.
