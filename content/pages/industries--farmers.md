## Accounting built around crop and livestock operations

A generic small-business bookkeeper treats every client the same way. Farm accounting doesn't work like that, because input costs, commodity timing, and herd economics don't behave like a retail shop's revenue.

### Crop Operations
icon: ChartLine

Input costs for seed, fertilizer, and chemicals shift throughout the season, and commodity prices move daily. For row crop farmers, TruCount tracks cost per acre and times income recognition around grain sales and hedging activity, not a generic monthly close.

### Livestock Operations
icon: ClipboardCheck

Herd tracking, feed costs, and breeding cycles raise accounting questions a standard bookkeeper rarely sees. The team separates cow-calf and feeder cattle economics so you know which side of the operation is actually making money.

### Land and Equipment Depreciation
icon: Building2

Section 179 elections, bonus depreciation, and trade-in timing on tractors and combines affect your tax bill for years, not just one season. Depreciation schedules get modeled against your real cash flow, not just whatever saves the most this April.

## Tax strategies designed for multi-entity farm operations

### Entity Structuring for Multi-Entity Farms
icon: Building2

Many farm operations run a crop LLC, a cattle operation, and a separate equipment or land entity, each with different tax treatment. Entities get structured and coordinated so income, expenses, and liability sit where they should, not where they happened to land. See [entity type analysis](/services/entity-type-analysis) for how this works.

### R&D Tax Credit for Farmers
icon: Lightbulb

Testing new seed varieties, adjusting fertility programs, or developing irrigation techniques can qualify as research and development under federal tax code. Eligible activities get identified and documented so farms claim a credit most tax preparers never mention.

### Excess Fertility Deduction
icon: Sparkles

When you purchase farmland with fertility levels above the norm for the area, that excess fertility can often be deducted rather than capitalized. The calculation and documentation get built to hold up under IRS review.

### Multi-Entity Tax Planning
icon: Scale

Coordinating income and deductions across multiple entities takes planning before year-end, not after it. Read more about the firm's approach to [multi-entity farm tax strategy](/industries/agriculture/multi-entity-farm-tax-strategy).

## Planning for the next generation of your farm

Farms built over two or three generations don't transition smoothly by accident. If you're bringing a son, daughter, or next-generation partner into the operation, or you're the one stepping into land your parents worked, the entity structure and tax decisions made now determine how much of the farm actually survives the transfer.

TruCount helps family operations set up entities, buy-sell agreements, and gifting or transfer strategies that keep the operation intact and the tax bill manageable. This isn't a document signed once and filed away. The plan gets revisited as land values, family involvement, and tax law change, so it still fits your operation in ten years. If succession is somewhere on your horizon, [farm succession planning](/industries/agriculture/farm-succession-planning) is built specifically for operations like yours.
